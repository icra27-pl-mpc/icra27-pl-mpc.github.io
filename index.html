RESEARCH OVERVIEW

Abstract

Planning generates experience.
Learning shapes future plans.

Read the full manuscript ↗

Planning with learned world models combines online trajectory optimization with learned value and policy functions for high-dimensional control. The planner determines the experience used for learning, while the critic and actor score and propose future plans. Recent policy-constrained TD-MPC variants strengthen one part of this feedback loop by aligning the learned policy with planner behavior.

We introduce PL-MPC (Planning–Learning MPC), which additionally modifies critic supervision and planner terminal-value estimation. Hybrid multi-step TD targets expose critic updates to more realized rewards before bootstrapping. Adaptive terminal estimates use target-critic disagreement to reduce the influence of uncertain terminal values during MPPI planning. Return-weighted actor distillation emphasizes planner-executed actions from higher-return episodes. The world-model architecture and MPPI optimizer remain unchanged.

The largest gains over TD-M(PC)2 occur on HumanoidBench balance-hard, where Total Average Return (TAR) increases from 98 ± 18 to 387 ± 255, and hurdle, from 199 ± 13 to 466 ± 200. Broader benchmark performance remains task dependent, and PL-MPC remains competitive on DMControl. Controlled ablations show different component interactions across the two tasks. We further demonstrate zero-shot sim-to-real wrench–nut alignment on a 7-DoF KUKA iiwa14, with higher observed success than TD-M(PC)2 on the training object size and two unseen sizes.

03 THE ARCHITECTURE

Close the
planning–learning loop.

PL-MPC extends the policy-constrained TD-M(PC)² backbone at three interfaces, using the same latent world model, critic architecture, and MPPI optimizer.

From plans to experience, and back.

Planning generates the experience that shapes future plans. The actor proposes candidate actions, and MPPI scores short model rollouts using predicted rewards and a terminal critic value. Replayed experience updates the world model, critic, and actor, which return to the planner for the next decision.

Hybrid multi-step TD targets (MTD) bring later observed rewards directly into earlier critic targets. Rewards from planner-generated replay are used where available. When a target extends beyond the sampled replay slice, the learned model predicts the remaining rewards and states under the current actor, followed by a terminal critic bootstrap.

Adaptive terminal estimates (ATE) use disagreement among target critics to penalize the online-critic terminal value at the end of each candidate rollout. MPPI uses this adjusted value to rank trajectories, reducing reliance on uncertain estimates. The TD targets remain unchanged.

Return-weighted actor distillation (RAD) adds imitation of planner-executed actions, weighted by the source episode’s realized return rather than the current critic’s estimate. A fixed warmup delays this additional loss, allowing useful planner experience to accumulate while the backbone actor objective remains active.

Evaluation protocol+

Humanoid control

Each seed’s TAR averages its final five evaluations; tables report mean ± SD across seeds. HumanoidBench comparisons and component ablations use three seeds. The main benchmark uses one fixed PL-MPC configuration, including a 200K-step RAD warmup.

Zero-shot hardware deployment

One independently trained policy is evaluated per method. Checkpoints are selected in simulation within the first 500K training steps. Hardware success requires seating the wrench around the nut before the 128-step limit; trial counts and 95% Wilson intervals appear in the hardware table.

A separate 200-episode simulation evaluation on the training size gives 81.0% success for PL-MPC and 67.5% for TD-M(PC)2. These episodes are not used for checkpoint selection.

07 TAKE-HOME POINTS

Closing the loop
beyond policy alignment.

The planner creates experience. What the critic and actor learn from it shapes the next plan.

Three interfaces, one planning–learning loop.

PL-MPC modifies critic supervision with MTD, terminal-value evaluation with ATE, and planner-to-policy transfer with RAD, while retaining the world-model architecture and MPPI optimizer.

Successful behaviors on difficult humanoid tasks.

The largest gains occur on balance-hard and hurdle, where successful seeds achieve full-episode balancing and hurdle traversal. Outcomes remain seed dependent, and the ablations show that the mechanisms interact differently across tasks.

Zero-shot transfer to hardware and unseen sizes.

The simulation-trained world model supports online planning for real-robot wrench–nut alignment, with higher observed success than TD-M(PC)2 on the training size and both unseen sizes, without real-world fine-tuning.

Scope and future directions+

Return-weighted distillation has limited signal when useful planner trajectories remain rare, as on maze. RAD is also sensitive to its activation schedule, motivating adaptive warmup. The mechanisms have been evaluated on the TD-M(PC)2 backbone; systematic transfer to other TD-MPC-style methods remains future work.
